What Is TwitterScore? How the Score Works and Why It Matters

By Unique Labs · November 14, 2026 · 6 min read

TwitterScore is a third-party rating for X (Twitter) accounts. It gives every account a number from 0 to 1000, and that number is mostly about one thing: who follows you, not how many people do.

The short answer

A normal follower count treats every follower the same. TwitterScore does not. It looks at whether respected accounts in a niche (founders, funds, researchers, well-known builders and influencers, mostly in crypto and tech) follow you, and weighs those follows far more than anonymous ones. Two accounts with 10,000 followers can have very different scores.

What the score measures

  • Quality of your followers. A follow from an account that itself has a high score counts for much more than a follow from a new or inactive account.
  • How many notable accounts follow you. These are often called smart followers. The site lists them on your profile page.
  • Standing inside a niche. The score was built around crypto Twitter, so it reflects reputation in that community better than general fame.

TwitterScore does not publish its full formula, so treat any exact recipe you read online with caution. What is consistent across accounts is the pattern: gain follows from high-score accounts and your score rises; gain thousands of random followers and it barely moves.

What the score ignores

  • Raw follower count. Bought or botted followers do not help, and a sudden wave of low-quality followers can make an account look worse to people checking it.
  • Likes and impressions on a single post. One viral post does not change who follows you.
  • How long you have had the account, on its own.

Why people check it

In crypto and startup circles the score is used as a quick trust filter:

  • Projects check it before choosing ambassadors, KOLs and whitelist winners.
  • Investors and founders glance at it before replying to a cold message.
  • Campaign platforms sometimes use influence scores to weight rewards.
  • You can use it to judge whether an account is worth your attention before you spend time on it.

It is a signal, not a verdict. Plenty of thoughtful people have low scores because they are new, and a high score says nothing about whether someone is honest.

What is a good TwitterScore?

There is no official grading. As a rough guide from how people talk about it: accounts in the low double digits are effectively unknown, a few hundred means some notable accounts follow you, and scores above 500 belong to accounts most people in the niche already recognise. That is why ScoreX treats 500 as the line between its two target tiers.

TwitterScore and other influence scores

TwitterScore is not the only one. Sorsa (formerly TweetScout), Ethos and Moni publish their own scores with their own inputs. They often agree on who is influential and disagree on the exact ranking, so it helps to watch more than one. ScoreX can show your TwitterScore, Sorsa, Ethos and Moni scores side by side and track them over time.

How to raise it

Because the score follows your followers, the only durable way up is to earn follows from accounts that already score well. That means being visible and useful where they are: in the replies under their posts. Frequently asked questions

Is TwitterScore made by X?

No. TwitterScore is an independent site. It reads public X data and is not affiliated with X Corp.

Does buying followers raise my TwitterScore?

No. The score weighs who follows you, so low-quality or fake followers add little or nothing.

How often does TwitterScore update?

The site refreshes accounts on its own schedule, so a new follow from a notable account may take a while to show. Checking once a day is enough.
