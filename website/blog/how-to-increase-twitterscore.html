How to Increase Your TwitterScore: 9 Things That Actually Move It

By Unique Labs · November 14, 2026 · 8 min read

Your TwitterScore goes up when accounts that already have a high score follow you. Everything in this guide is about earning those follows honestly and repeatedly.

1. Understand what moves the number

The score is driven by smart followers: notable accounts in your niche. A hundred of them matter more than fifty thousand strangers. If a tactic does not lead to a respected account choosing to follow you, it will not move your score. See What Is TwitterScore? for the background.

2. Build a target list, not a wish list

Write down the accounts you want to notice you. Good targets are active, post original thoughts, and still follow people back sometimes. Mix sizes: a few big names for reach, and many mid-sized accounts with real standing, because they notice replies and follow back more often.

3. Check who is likely to follow back

An account with 100,000 followers that follows 2,000 people almost never adds one more. An account with 8,000 followers that follows 3,000 does it every week. Look at the follower-to-following ratio before you invest effort. ScoreX skips accounts that follow under 5% of their followers for this reason.

4. Reply early, and reply with something

Replies are the one place a small account can sit next to a large one. Early replies get read by the author and by everyone who opens the post. But only if the reply adds something: a sharp question, an implication the post left out, an honest doubt, a concrete example. "Great post" is invisible. More on this in How to Write Replies That Authors Actually Answer.

5. Follow before you reply

When an author sees a good reply, they often click the profile. If you already follow them, that small signal of interest is in place. It costs nothing and it removes one reason not to follow back.

6. Make your profile worth following

A reply earns the click. The profile earns the follow. In ten seconds a visitor should understand what you do and what they will get from following you:

  • A clear bio that says what you work on, without buzzwords.
  • A pinned post that shows your best thinking.
  • Recent posts of your own, so you are not only a replier.
  • A real avatar and a banner that is not empty.

7. Answer when they answer

If an author replies to you, that is the best moment to earn the follow. Answer what they said, briefly and directly. Do not let it sit for a day. ScoreX can draft or post these answers for you so a conversation never goes cold.

8. Stay consistent for weeks

One good reply rarely earns a follow. Being a familiar, useful name under someone's posts for two weeks often does. Decide how many replies you can sustain every day and keep that pace. Bursts followed by silence look like a campaign.

9. Track follow-backs and adjust

Keep a record of who followed back, after how many replies, and which kinds of reply came before it. Drop accounts that have not responded after a fair try, and add new ones. Over a month you will see which tiers and tones work for you. ScoreX keeps this record automatically, including each follower's TwitterScore.

What does not work

  • Buying followers or engagement. It does not raise the score and it damages trust.
  • Follow-for-follow groups. The accounts in them rarely have scores worth having.
  • Mass generic replies. They get muted, and X limits accounts that behave like spam.

How long does it take?

It depends on your niche, your profile and how well you write. Expect weeks, not days, and expect uneven progress: nothing for a while, then two or three notable follows close together. Nobody can promise a number, and you should be wary of anyone who does.

Frequently asked questions

What is the fastest way to increase TwitterScore?

Earn follows from accounts that already have a high score. In practice that means consistent, specific replies under their posts and a profile that gives them a reason to follow.

Do likes and retweets raise my TwitterScore?

Not directly. They help your posts get seen, which can lead to follows from notable accounts, and those follows are what move the score.

Can a tool raise my score for me?

No tool can guarantee follows. A tool like ScoreX can make the daily work consistent and show you what is working; the follow is still the other person's decision.
