Crypto Twitter Growth Guide for Small Accounts

By Unique Labs · November 14, 2026 · 8 min read

Crypto Twitter (CT) runs on reputation. Who follows you opens doors that follower count never will: early access, ambassador roles, honest answers from founders. This guide is for accounts starting small.

How CT is different

  • It is a small town. A few thousand accounts set the conversation, and they notice who shows up consistently.
  • Reputation is measured. Projects check influence scores like TwitterScore before choosing collaborators.
  • Trust is fragile. One shilled scam can undo a year of good posts.

Step 1: Pick a lane

"Crypto" is too wide. Choose something you can be specific about: a chain, DeFi mechanics, on-chain data, security, a single ecosystem, memecoin culture, regulation. People follow accounts they can describe in one sentence.

Step 2: Set up a profile that passes the ten-second test

  • Bio: what you look at and why you are worth reading. No "DYOR | NFA | WAGMI" lists.
  • Pinned post: your best original piece of thinking.
  • A consistent avatar. People recognise the picture before the name.

Step 3: Find the right 100 people

Do not start from the biggest names. Start from the smart followers of two or three accounts you respect in your lane. They are the active middle of the community: big enough to matter, small enough to read replies. Check each one's follower-to-following ratio so you spend effort on people who still follow back.

Step 4: Live in the replies

For a small account, replies are the product. Be early under posts from your list and add something: a question about the mechanism, a risk they did not mention, an example. The method is in The Reply Strategy and the wording in How to Write Replies That Authors Actually Answer.

Step 5: Post your own work twice a week

Replies bring visitors; your posts convince them. Good formats for small accounts:

  • One chart or one transaction, explained.
  • A short thread on how something actually works.
  • What you got wrong last month and what you learned.
  • A clear summary of a governance proposal or a long report.

Step 6: Protect your reputation

  • Do not shill what you have not checked. Say when you hold something.
  • Disclose paid posts. Hidden promotion is found out sooner or later.
  • Never post or click wallet-drainer links, and be suspicious of DMs offering paid collaborations to tiny accounts.
  • Stay out of pile-ons. They bring followers you do not want.

Step 7: Track what matters

Weekly, look at three things: which notable accounts followed you, which replies got an answer from the author, and how your score moved. Ignore impressions on single posts. Traps to avoid

  • Engagement pods and follow-for-follow. They inflate numbers nobody respects.
  • Buying followers. It does not move influence scores and it is visible to anyone who looks.
  • Copy-paste replies under every post. You will be muted, and X limits accounts that do it. See automation safety.
  • Chasing airdrop farms. A reputation as a farmer is hard to lose.

A 30-day plan

  1. Days 1 to 3: choose your lane, fix your profile, write a pinned post.
  2. Days 4 to 7: build a list of 100 accounts; follow them.
  3. Weeks 2 to 4: 15 to 30 good replies a day, two original posts a week, answer every reply you get.
  4. End of month: review follow-backs, retire silent targets, add new ones.

Results vary, and nobody can promise a number. What this plan guarantees is that the right people see you often enough to form an opinion.

Frequently asked questions

How many followers do I need to be taken seriously on Crypto Twitter?

Fewer than you think. A small account followed by respected people is taken more seriously than a large one followed by nobody notable.

Should I use my real name?

Many respected CT accounts are pseudonymous. Consistency and a track record matter more than a real name.

How often should I post?

A couple of original posts a week plus daily replies is enough to start. Quality and regularity beat volume.
