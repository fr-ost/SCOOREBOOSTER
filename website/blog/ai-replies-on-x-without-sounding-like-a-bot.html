AI Replies on X: How to Use AI Without Sounding Like a Bot

By Unique Labs · November 14, 2026 · 7 min read

Everyone on X has learned to spot an AI reply. It praises the post, restates it, and ends with a question nobody would ask. Used that way, AI costs you reputation. Used well, it is a fast first draft in your own voice.

Why AI replies get spotted

  • Empty agreement. "Great insight! This really highlights..."
  • Restating the post in slightly different words.
  • Assistant vocabulary. "Intriguing", "insightful", "it's important to note", "game changer".
  • Perfect polish. Full sentences, tidy punctuation, an em dash, no personality.
  • The same shape every time. Compliment, summary, question.
  • Forced slang. A model told to "sound casual" overdoes it.

Brief it like a person, not like a tool

The single biggest improvement is describing your own voice in your own words: how you talk, how long you write, what you never say. A few lines are enough:

Dry, direct, lowercase. One thought, under 20 words. I ask about mechanisms, not about feelings. No hype, no emojis, never start with "great".

Then tell the model what the reply is for. "Write a reply" produces filler. "Ask one sharp question about their main claim" produces something an author might answer. The patterns are in How to Write Replies That Authors Actually Answer.

Ban list

Models drift back to the same phrases. Forbid them explicitly, and check the output:

  • Openers: Great, Wow, Love, Interesting, Sounds like, Looks like, I think
  • Phrases: well said, this is huge, game changer, curious to see, excited to see, thanks for sharing, couldn't agree more
  • Format: links, hashtags, @mentions, em dashes, bullet points, quotation marks around the reply
  • Emojis that read as promotion: rocket, sparkles, hundred, target, chart

Vary the shape

Even good replies become a pattern if they all look alike. Change length, opening, capitalisation and approach from one reply to the next: sometimes a question, sometimes a doubt, sometimes an example. ScoreX draws these at random for every reply so consecutive ones never match.

Never let it make things up

A model will happily invent a statistic, a personal story or "when I worked at". On a public account, in your name, that is a real risk. Rules worth enforcing:

  • No numbers, names or events that are not in the post.
  • No claims about what you did, used, read or own.
  • Opinions, questions and general observations only.

Check before posting

A second pass catches most problems. Ask of every draft:

  1. Could this be posted under any post? If yes, discard it.
  2. Does it give the author something to answer?
  3. Is it in the same language as the post?
  4. Is it too similar to something I posted recently?
  5. Would I be comfortable if the author knew a model drafted it?

Stay in the loop

The safest setup is the one where you read each reply before it goes out, at least until you trust the voice. Edit freely: a tool that learns from your edits gets closer to you every week. Review mode also matters for your account's safety; see Is Automating Replies on X Safe?

What AI should not reply to

  • Grief, illness and personal news.
  • Politics and heated arguments.
  • Insults aimed at you. No reply is the right reply.
  • Anything where being wrong would hurt someone.

The standard to hold

A reply is good if the author would want to answer it and you would be happy to have written it. Whether a model typed the first draft matters less than whether a person stands behind the final one.

Frequently asked questions

Can people tell if a reply was written by AI?

Often, yes. Generic praise, restating the post and assistant vocabulary give it away. A specific reply in a consistent personal voice is much harder to tell apart.

Which AI model is best for X replies?

Any recent model works if the brief is good. Your voice description, a ban list and a quality check matter more than the model.

Should I disclose that I use AI for replies?

That is your call and may depend on your audience. What matters most is that you read and stand behind what is posted in your name.
